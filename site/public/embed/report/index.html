Ravens at Chiefs · 2024 week 1 · Mahomes to Noah Gray

Play 001, Graded

Mahomes took the snap under center at the Ravens' 24, dropped 7.5 yards and, 2.25 seconds after the snap, hit Noah Gray 8.1 yards downfield between Roquan Smith and Kyle Hamilton. Gray went down 1.6 yards later: a 9.7-yard gain to the Ravens' 14. What the tracking shows

  1. Won up front. Four rushers against five linemen and a back who likely chipped. Humphrey and Thuney double-teamed Travis Jones for the whole dropback, and the closest any Raven got to Mahomes was 2.8 m (Madubuike, at 2.1 s). The ball was out at 2.25 s.
  2. Two deep, and Gray sat under them. Both safeties bailed to about 17 yards by the throw, with Hamilton and Roquan Smith at 8.4 and 8.5. Gray settled at 8.4 yards in the gap between those two, 2.4 m from Smith.
  3. The throw took the yards, not the safest window. At the release Kelce (+0.22 s) and Pacheco (+0.58 s) were open but short, and Gray was tight (−0.11 s) at 8-plus yards. The two deeper routes were covered: Receiver B's vertical was capped by Eddie Jackson over the top (−0.39 s), and Receiver A by the right cornerback (−0.53 s). By this measure there was no open receiver deeper than Gray to find.
  4. Gray's window never quite opened; the ball beat it. By the reach model Gray came closest to open at 1.75 s (−0.02 s, 6.2 yards downfield), just after Mahomes hit the top of his drop at 1.5 s. Mahomes climbed a yard and threw at 2.25 s, two yards deeper into a slightly tighter window. The ball flew 0.53 s at an average 25.7 m/s, about 57 mph.
  5. Hamilton limited the damage. Gray caught it at 9 mph with Smith 2.0 m away. Hamilton reached him 0.37 s later, and Gray was down 0.75 s after the catch, 1.6 yards on, at the Ravens' 14.

What this report cannot see

  • Where anyone looked. The Film Room's first-person view turns each head toward the play by rule; it is not measured. So nothing here grades a read, a progression or a look-off. Measuring the helmet's facing on the endzone film is the step that would unlock it.
  • The play call and the assignments. Whether Gray was the first read, and which zone each Raven owned. Coverage grades therefore judge proximity, not responsibility.
  • Technique. Hands, pad level, footwork and the throwing motion. The bodies are fitted to 17 keypoints on players about 140 pixels tall, and the throw, catch and tackle poses in the renders are drawn from the play's events, not fitted.
  • Three numbers were never read on the film: Receivers A and B, and the Ravens' right cornerback.
  • The pile. Madubuike's position after the throw (frames 552 to 596) was read off the film by hand. Positions inside the pile are the least certain in the play, to about half a metre.

How it is measured

Data
The reconstruction: every player's spot on the turf at 59.94 frames a second, from the sideline and endzone All-22 cameras, each solved on every frame. The snap, release, catch and down frames are read off the film.
Accuracy
Typically within about 0.3 m where both cameras see a player, and up to about 1 m across the field where only the sideline camera does.
Separation
Straight-line distance on the turf from a receiver to the nearest Raven.
Reach margin
For a throw at that instant, the ball flies at this throw's own average speed (25.7 m/s) to where the receiver will be. Each Raven runs straight at that spot at 7.0 m/s after a 0.2 s reaction, and can contest within 1 m. The margin is the first Raven's time minus the ball's time. It ranks the options against each other; it is not a completion probability. On the one throw that can be checked, it calls Gray −0.11 s, and the pass was complete.
Pressure
A Raven within 2 m of the quarterback before the throw.
Grades
Per player, per play, from what the positions show. There is no play call and only one play, so there is no population to rank against. Treat them as a structured reading of the tracking, not a film grade.
